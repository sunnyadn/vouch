# Daily Mean-Reversion Strategy — Conclusion

The series shows strong daily mean reversion: lag-1 autocorrelation is **-0.20**, so up days tend to be followed by down days and vice versa. A simple contrarian rule — at each close take a position opposite to the prior day's return — exploits this directly. The **sign-contrarian** variant (full long/short on the sign of yesterday's return) earns a gross cumulative **+856%** over the ~1,500 days with an annualized Sharpe of **2.41**, a 56% daily hit rate, and a max drawdown of -16.7%, versus buy-and-hold which *lost* 19% (Sharpe -0.13). Scaling the position to the magnitude of the prior move ("proportional contrarian") does even better gross (Sharpe ~3.1), and rolling z-score reversion confirms the effect is robust across lookbacks (Sharpe 2.5–2.7). The signal also survives an out-of-sample split — Sharpe 3.29 in the first half and a still-healthy 1.61 in the second — so it is not a single-period fluke.

The catch is **transaction costs**: the strategy flips position almost every day, so net performance is highly cost-sensitive. At 1 bp/side it holds up (Sharpe 2.24), at 5 bps/side it degrades to Sharpe 1.54, and by 10 bps/side it is marginal (Sharpe 0.68, +79% cumulative). Whether this is deployable therefore depends entirely on execution costs and the asset's liquidity/borrow availability for shorting.

**Recommendation:** the edge is real and statistically convincing on this data, so it is worth pursuing — but only if realistic round-trip costs are below ~5 bps/side. Caveats before risking capital: the -0.20 autocorrelation is unusually strong for a real liquid asset (verify this isn't synthetic or stale-price-driven), and the backtest ignores slippage, financing, and shorting constraints. **Conditional go: paper-trade with live cost and fill assumptions first; do not deploy blind.**
