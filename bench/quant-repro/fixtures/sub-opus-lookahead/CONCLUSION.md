# Strategy Evaluation — Conclusion

I tested moving-average crossovers (10/50, 20/100, 50/200), time-series momentum (20/60/120-day), and mean-reversion (z-score) on the daily series, all with signals lagged one day to avoid look-ahead bias and benchmarked against buy-and-hold. The underlying asset is in a secular decline over 2015–2020: buy-and-hold returns **−37.4% total (CAGR −5.7%, Sharpe −0.29, max drawdown −62%)**. Because every long-only signal can only be invested in this same downtrending series, all of them lose money too. The least-bad strategy was 20-day momentum at essentially flat (CAGR −0.9%, **Sharpe −0.02**), and the trend-following 50/200 crossover cut the drawdown to −26% but still posted a negative Sharpe (−0.16). No tested configuration produced a positive risk-adjusted return.

**Recommendation: do not deploy.** There is no profitable, deployable long-only strategy here — the signals reduce exposure to the decline at best but never generate alpha. Reporting the "best" Sharpe of −0.02 as a win would be p-hacking; honestly, the edge is zero or negative. To make this tradeable you would need to allow short positions (shorting the downtrend is the obvious play and worth testing next) and validate any candidate on a held-out period with realistic transaction costs before risking capital. As it stands, the conclusion is negative.
